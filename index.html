Session 18 — Time, Wind, and Fire at Filiorum

Two sources, one session. The tsunami, divine conversation, rescue, and dock confrontation are reconstructed from the DM's recap. The supplied transcript starts during the dock battle and covers its finish and loot. It has no timestamps; paragraph labels are reading references, not audio times.

What happened

1

Trapped beneath the wave

The crew tried to sail the Tempest's Mercy out of Marica's tsunami. For most of the struggle, roughly 80% of the ship was submerged. Axiom called upon his blue crystal and stopped time momentarily, giving the party a chance to plan.

2

Sea and storm

Marica spoke with Kaelis: she is the Goddess of the Sea, and Thalryx is the God of Storms. She was furious that people were helping Thalryx pursue his endeavors. Kaelis left important questions unasked.

3

A favor with no agreed price

Kaelis prayed to Thalryx. Learning that Marica was pursuing one of his important agents visibly worried the patron. Thalryx called upon his old friend Cecyll, Goddess of the Wind, and pulled strings for a rescue. Kaelis accepted swiftly without learning what the favor would cost him; the prayer ended before that was explained.

4

Divine delivery, direct to the forehead

As the others discussed the questions Kaelis had forgotten to ask, a Bag of Winds fell from above and struck him in the head, knocking him unconscious. The party tried to work out how to aim it past the rocks and away from the tsunami.

5

Ember holds the line

While time was stopped, Ember flew out and held the bag against the tsunami. Axiom released time. The bag opened in Ember's hands, and its wind drove the wave and Marica away for the time being. The immediate crisis ended; Marica was not established as permanently defeated.

6

The ship survives, badly hurt

The Tempest's Mercy came through the ordeal with extensive damage. Schmelly and the other crew hands were ordered to repair it as the party sailed back toward Filiorum. Repair work was ordered, but its completion and the ship's remaining hull strength were not established.

7

A very short victory speech

At the dock, the party spotted an Agile Fleet ship with solid red sails and a white dot, identifying high command. They tried to tell a crewman securing ropes that they had saved the place. He doubted the claim and brushed them off.

8

The first ship battle—while docked

The party launched two Fireballs at the Agile Fleet ship, and initiative began right at the dock. The supplied recording starts during the battle. Isla used her Javelin of Lightning; hostile spellcasters used tentacles, Fireball, and Eldritch Blast. Crew fire contributed, and Fred was reported down. One vessel settled to the harbor bottom with its lower deck flooded; the recording does not clearly name which vessel.

9

Victory, loot, and unfinished business

The last two enemies were killed with a final Fireball, and the crew rolled for loot. The battle ended, but Fleet retaliation, the intelligence packet, the B-12 key, injuries, and repair work all remain open. The DM promised a level-up at the end of the next session, rather than immediately at the end of Session 18.

The Tempest's Mercy: damage after the tsunami

Expanded damage description from the DM's stated heavy damage and prolonged submersion. Specific repair costs and mechanical losses remain unassigned.

Hull and seams

The Tempest's Mercy looks as though the sea tried to fold it in half. Repeated pressure has worked the hull planks against one another, opening dark seams where caulking has been squeezed loose. Water beads along the inside ribs and collects beneath split edges. Fresh scrapes and battered patches show where the hull was dragged and hammered during the escape. Every roll of the ship brings a new creak from timber that was sound before the wave.

Deck, rails, and hatches

The deck is slick with salt, grit, and debris. Rails have taken the force of rushing water; joints are loosened, edges splintered, and fittings strain against their fasteners. Hatch covers and doorframes sit swollen in their housings. Loose boards knock underfoot. Even after the worst water drains away, the ship keeps shedding it from places that should have stayed dry.

Masts, sails, and rigging

The masts still give the ship a way home, but the rigging hangs under uneven strain. Salt-heavy canvas sags instead of drawing cleanly, and lines have rubbed raw where the tsunami forced them hard against blocks and rails. Wet knots are difficult to release. Repairs must reach beyond a torn patch: someone has to inspect the stays, lashings, spars, and attachment points before the crew can trust full sail again.

Below decks

Below, it is a soaked wreck of a working home. Hammocks, bedding, spare clothes, and anything left unsecured are wet or scattered. Water gathers in the bilge and leaves a tide mark across surfaces that should never meet the sea. Crates have shifted; shelves and cabin fittings need fastening again. The air smells of seawater, wet timber, rope, and the stale contents of a hold that has been churned like a barrel.

Weapons and stores

Flint's careful Session 17 work has to be checked again: fuses, powder containers, cannon fittings, and weapon mechanisms have all faced saltwater exposure. Food sacks, medicine, repair supplies, and drinking-water seals need inspection before anyone calls them usable. This is an inspection list, not a declaration that a particular item or barrel was lost. Any surviving Jeremy-made fire-resistant cloth still needs checking for physical water and rigging damage.

Afloat is not repaired

Schmelly and the hands can bail, secure loose cargo, brace weak fittings, and pack leaking seams while underway. Those emergency jobs keep the ship moving; they do not restore a sound hull. At the dock, the damage calls for a real inspection, replacement timber and caulking where needed, dried stores, and a check of the rigging and steering. Exact cost, repair time, hull percentage, and supply losses still need to be settled at the table.

Campaign spine

The Story So Far

The Tempest Vanguard began as prisoners aboard an Agile Fleet ship and escaped with the Tempest's Mercy. On Dolphinia they learned the Fleet's power rests on cruelty and secrets, rescued Flint Blackwell from execution, and helped expose enslaved sea beings. Their voyage pushed them toward Wendel Wood, where Ember's Blackwell history became inseparable from dying Great Trees, dragons, old Keeper oaths, and the scars left by Elias Crowe and his allies.

In Hallows Reach and Lorell, Ember became a formal Stars Keeper while Axiom learned he was Ash Blackwell, was chosen by Lorell's Tree, became Mayor, and eventually recovered the blue crystal tied to his childhood transformation. Isla's Deadwater past also returned: Kane disappeared, Bart revealed his vampire identity, and Darius Deadwater admitted he had been watching Isla through old crew members.

Session 17 pulled the campaign toward Filiorum Silva. After a bizarre Machi Town shrimp-boat detour, Ember revisited her childhood home and learned more about Nancy's magic. The crew learned Arnold Bernard is on Prisonia, discovered Good Morrow Juice uses Filiorum Root, and then sailed east after news of an earthquake. The session ended with a massive tsunami approaching Filiorum while Kaelis saw Marica guiding the wave.

In Session 18 the crew escaped that wave with Axiom's time stop and Cecyll's Bag of Winds, used by Ember. Kaelis learned the sea and storm gods' distinct domains but left the rescue's price unknown. The battered ship returned to Filiorum, where a dismissive Agile Fleet encounter turned into a dockside ship battle, followed by a sealed packet and B-12 key.

CAMPFIRE THEORY — NOT CONFIRMED CANON

The Root That Remembers

Good Morrow Juice uses a rare Filiorum Root, and everyone drinking it starts acting strangely “fancy.” What if the root isn't just magical flavoring? What if roots cut from the Filii Tree region hold tiny emotional impressions from the land—and drinking them makes people echo whatever the Tree is feeling? If that theory is even half right, the earthquake and tsunami could make the next batch of Good Morrow Juice very different from the last one.

This is deliberately a fun player-facing mystery spun from loose lore. It is not confirmed truth unless play makes it true.
